Station UE 13

Flow on the platform and the train run

You have a flow, a board, a tray, a sign and a map. This lesson wires them into one game: each state shows the right things, a won board sends a train through the track you laid, and an unfinished level comes back as you left it.

At the end
A shell actor that builds and conducts everything, FTsugiTrackPath in TsugiCore, and a train actor.
On the headset
The whole loop with no console: network, line, station, play, the train, arrival, next station.
Unity equivalent
Milestone XR7, "Flow on the platform", and the train from XR4.

Unreal concepts

Who owns what

In Unity, XRGame owns the flow and the things on show. In your port those are split along Unreal's own lines:

Lives inLifetimeHolds
UTsugiGameSubsystemThe appFlow, save, preferences, level list
ATsugiShell, an actor in L_TsugiThe levelThe board, map, sign, tray, piece hands, train, steam; and the reaction to each state
ATsugiPawnThe levelHead, hands, grab input
ATsugiBoardPlacementThe levelWhere the board root is in the room

The shell is the port of XRGame.Build and XRGame.OnStateChanged. It decides nothing about rules; it shows and hides.

Spawning actors and finding each other

GetWorld()->SpawnActor<T>(Class, Transform) creates an actor at runtime. Spawn from a TSubclassOf<T> property so the Blueprint subclass, with its art assigned, is what gets created. Attach spawned actors to the board root with AttachToComponent so they move with the board.

Avoid searching the level for actors by class each frame. Have the shell hold pointers to what it spawned, and have the level contain exactly one shell that looks up the pawn and the placement once in BeginPlay.

Animation without coroutines

Unity code animates with coroutines: a function that yields each frame. Unreal has no direct equivalent in C++. Pick by what the motion is:

MotionUnreal toolHere
Continuous, driven by stateTick, with FMath::FInterpTo or your own mathsThe train, signs turning to face the head
A fixed short tweenA small struct holding start time and duration, advanced in Tick; or FTimeline with a curve assetA piece scaling in (220 ms), a clue popping (160 ms)
Something springyUKismetMathLibrary::FloatSpringInterp with an FFloatSpringStateThe train's squash and stretch
Wait, then do one thingA timerThe falling piece's puff
Authored UI motionA UMG widget animationThe arrival stamp

The Unity phone game animates on unscaled time because it pauses with timeScale. The XR edition pauses by stopping the flow's clock and locking input, not by stopping time, so ordinary delta time is fine.

Steps

  1. Build the shell. It waits for the board to be placed, creates everything under the board root, connects events, and starts the flow. This is XRGame.Start and Build.

    The class properties are TSubclassOf<ATsugiBoard> BoardClass and so on, set in BP_TsugiShell to your Blueprint subclasses. Delete the board you placed in the level by hand in lesson 7; the shell spawns it now.

  2. React to every state. Compare this with the Unity method. The structure is the same; that is the point of having ported the flow unchanged.

    Every button on the sign and every roundel goes through one guard, the port of When: act only if the flow is still in the state that drew the button.

  3. Load a level, and resume one. OnLevelStarted carries the saved attempt, if there is one. Apply it with FTsugiLevelProgress::ApplyTo, which writes cells with SetUnchecked. The flow has already restored the clock to the saved time, idle, waiting for the first grab.

  4. Port TrackPath. It joins the solved board's curves into one route measured by arc length, with a one-cell straight into each tunnel so the train starts and ends out of sight. Sample(distance) finds the segment by binary search and returns a position and a tangent; beyond either end it continues straight. It belongs in TsugiCore and comes with seven tests.

  5. Run the train. One actor under the board, one static mesh component per car: a locomotive and seven wagons, the kit's E235 models at 0.3 scale.

    RevealDistance comes from the board: about a third of the way into the tunnel tube. Hiding is a visibility switch, not clipping: an eight-car train is far longer than any tunnel that fits on the platform.

    The shell answers OnFinished by calling Flow.FinishTrainRun(), guarded so it only does so in the TrainRun state.

  6. Connect play to the flow.

  7. Tick the small things. In the shell's Tick: set bAcceptsGrabs (Play, a board loaded, handle not moving); make the handle available except in Pause and while a piece is held; update the sign's clock from Flow.Timer and Flow.GetStarTier() in Play and Pause.

  8. Add the quick-test aid. Port QuickBoard and expose it as a console variable, Tsugi.QuickRails, default 0. With a value of 3, all but three rails of each board are pre-laid in memory, so a station plays in seconds. Never apply it to the tutorial station. The Unity scene ships with this at 3; do not copy that.

Your port

  • FTsugiTrackPath and TrackPathTests.
  • FTsugiQuickBoard and QuickBoardTests.
  • The rest of the shell: ShowLineViews with a mark for each station (open, in progress, stars, next), ShowStationSign, ShowPausedSign, ShowArrivalSign, ClearBoard, TotalStars, the map's two handlers.
  • The destination plate above the locomotive: the station's name followed by 行 ("bound for"). This is environment art in Japanese, like the platform-edge decals, and needs a font with those glyphs.
  • Stretch goal, after everything else works: the hand on the train. A fingertip within about a centimetre of a car shakes it, pushes the train up to three times its speed, and squashes and stretches the cars on a loose spring. The constants are at the top of XRTrainRun.cs.

Acceptance checks

  • Tsugi.Core.TrackPath and QuickBoard tests pass.
  • With no console input: place the board, press line TS, press Ashgate, and the board, tray and station sign appear.
  • The clock on the sign stays at 00:00 until I first take a piece.
  • Completing the board hides the tray and sign and runs the train from S to E along my track, appearing inside one tunnel and vanishing inside the other.
  • Each car faces along the track through every curve.
  • After the train, the arrival shows my time and stars; NEXT starts the next station; at a line's ninth station it returns to the network.
  • Leaving a half-built level by quitting the app and returning shows "continue" state on the map, and choosing it restores my pieces and the clock.
  • RETRY from the arrival gives a clean board and a clock at zero.
  • Pressing a sign button twice quickly never produces an ensure in the log.
  • Tsugi.QuickRails is 0 in every config file in source control.

Pitfalls

  • Delegates that outlive their listener. The subsystem's flow lives for the whole app; the shell lives for the level. Remove handlers in EndPlay, or bind with AddUObject, which skips destroyed objects. Do both.
  • Order inside one state change. On Retry, the flow raises the state change to Play and then OnLevelStarted. The state handler must not assume the board is loaded yet. That is why "begin the piece hands" in the Play case only happens when coming from Pause.
  • The handle's footprint. The rail wraps the near corner of whatever is on show. A 6×6 board's corner is nearer the middle than the map card's. Call FitHandle on every change, or the rail floats beside nothing.
  • A train on a board with no route. The debug win path can reach TrainRun on an unsolved board. The train then waits 1.5 seconds and finishes, so the flow never hangs. Keep that branch.

Knowledge check

Why does the shell spawn BoardClass, a TSubclassOf<ATsugiBoard>, and not ATsugiBoard itself?

  • C++ classes cannot be spawned.
  • The Blueprint subclass is where the meshes and materials are assigned; spawning the C++ class directly gives a board with no art.
  • It is faster.

C++ declares the slots, the Blueprint fills them. Spawn the Blueprint.

A car's path sample has tangent (east 1, north 0). What is its yaw in Unreal?

  • 0°
  • 90°
  • −90°

North is local +X, yaw 0. East is local +Y, a quarter turn: atan2(east, north) = atan2(1, 0) = 90°. It matches BoardLayout.Yaw(East).

The Unity phone game lets the player tap to skip the train. Why does XR not?

  • Skipping is impossible without a touchscreen.
  • On the headset, a stray pinch just after the winning drop kept triggering the skip.
  • The train is shorter in XR.

The skip was removed after the XR7 headset check. It is a design decision recorded in the spec, so do not add one back as a convenience.
