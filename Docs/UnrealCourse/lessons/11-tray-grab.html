Tray and grab

The game becomes playable. You port the rules of picking up and putting down, implement the grab interface on your hand tracker, and build the tray, the held piece, the ghost, and everything that happens to a piece once it leaves the hand.

At the end
FTsugiPieceDrop and its helpers in TsugiCore with about 60 tests; a grab component, a tray, and a piece-hands component in TsugiXR.
On the headset
Pinch a piece from the tray, see a green or red ghost over the board, let go. Solve Ashgate by hand.
Unity equivalent
Milestones XR3 and XR6.

The interaction, exactly

A hand holds at most one piece. Both hands may hold at once. The tray has six slots, one per key, and never runs out.

You doOutcomeWhat you see and hear
Pinch a tray slotTakenThe piece is in your hand. Grab cue.
Pinch a piece you laidLiftedThe cell empties at once and the clues update. Lifting is erasing.
Pinch a fixed pieceRefused (FixedPiece)It wobbles. "That's fixed" note.
Hold a piece within 10 cm above a cell(hover)A ghost on that cell: green if the drop would land, red if not. The board does not change.
Let go over a cell where the key is legalPlaced, or Moved if it came from another cellThe piece scales in on the cell.
Let go over one of your own pieces, where your key would be legal without itReplacedThe old piece puffs.
Let go where the key is illegalReturnedThe piece flies back in a quick arc to the tray, or to the cell it was lifted from. Error cue.
Let go off the platform, or above the hover bandPuffedThe piece falls into the room as a real body, bounces off your table, and puffs after 3 seconds.
Throw it (hand faster than 1.2 m/s)ThrownThe same, ending in a bigger burst of steam.

An illegal drop never costs the player anything. And the ghost is the whole contract of a release: a piece lands on the cell its ghost is over, and with no ghost showing, letting go is letting go off the platform.

Unreal concepts

Collision as a query tool

Every primitive component has collision settings: whether it takes part in queries, in physics, or both, and how it responds to each channel. A grab volume is a box set to Query Only that blocks nothing. You never trace against it; you ask how far a point is from it.

Chaos physics

A primitive component becomes a rigid body with SetSimulatePhysics(true). It needs a collision shape of its own; the baked track meshes have none, so a falling piece is a small actor whose root is a box. Bounciness comes from a physical material asset. Fast, thin objects need continuous collision detection, or they pass through a table between two steps.

Niagara

Niagara is Unreal's particle system. A system asset is authored in the editor; code spawns it at a place with UNiagaraFunctionLibrary::SpawnSystemAtLocation and can set user parameters on it. The Unity steam is one particle system emitting either a puff or a burst. Yours can be one Niagara system with a user parameter for the amount.

Timers

Where Unity starts a coroutine to wait three seconds, Unreal sets a timer: GetWorldTimerManager().SetTimer(Handle, this, &AThing::Function, 3.f). For "destroy this actor after a while" there is SetLifeSpan.

Steps

  1. Port the rules and their tests first. Nothing here touches the engine.

    FTsugiPieceDrop holds a reference to the board the display owns: FTsugiBoard& Board. It must not outlive that board, so it is recreated on every level start and reset on teardown. The tutorial's Gate is a TFunction<bool(int32, int32, ETsugiPieceKey)>.

  2. Implement the grab interface. A component on the pawn, beside the hands. It keeps a list of targets and, on the frame a hand's pinch closes, gives that hand the nearest target that has something to give.

    From the headset checksThe comment about a hold carrying on is a ported lesson. In Unity, the toolkit asked "may this still be selected?" on every frame, and a cell that had just been emptied by the lift answered no, ending the grab the instant it began.

  3. Give the board its grab volumes. In ATsugiBoard, create one UBoxComponent per cell: the slab's footprint, reaching from the slab top to half a cell above it. Set Query Only, ignore all channels, no overlap events. Expose GetCellVolume(X, Y).

  4. Build the tray. Six slots in a 2×3 block beside the board, off the dominant-hand side of the edge the player stands at. Each slot shows its piece and has a grab volume registered with FTsugiGrabTarget::Tray(Key). Positions come from FTsugiTrayDock::SlotCentre, in the board's frame, so Tsugi::ToLocal places them.

    Every frame, give the dock the head's position in the board's frame. When the player has stood nearer another edge for 1.5 seconds, and no piece is held, the tray moves there. The layout puts each piece where it points when seen from the south edge:

  5. Port the piece hands. This component listens to the grab interface, calls the rules, and shows the result. Start with what happens while a piece is held.

    This is the inverse of Tsugi::ToLocal, and the only other place the axis swap appears. Put it in TsugiUnits.h as Tsugi::FromLocal and call that.

  6. Show the ghost. While a piece is held, ask PieceDrop.Hover(Hand, Cell) each frame. None hides the ghost. Legal and Illegal show it on the cell: the piece's mesh plus a thin slab, both in a translucent unlit material tinted green or red. Make one dynamic material instance per tint at start and swap them; do not create materials every frame.

  7. Play each outcome. The board has already changed by the time this runs. This is only the show.

    OutcomeThe held piece
    Placed, MovedDestroyed. Sync(true) scales the landed piece in.
    ReplacedDestroyed. Puff at the cell.
    Returned to a cellHide the piece the display already shows there, fly the held one back in an arc (0.25 s, rising 5 cm), then show it.
    Returned to the trayArc back to its slot.
    ThrownFalls with the hand's velocity. Ends in a burst.
    PuffedFalls. Ends in a puff.

  8. Make pieces fall.

    ThrowGesture works in metres per second, with a threshold of 1.2. Feed it positions in metres (centimetres divided by 100) so its constants and tests port unchanged, and multiply its velocity by 100 when you hand it to physics.

    For the board to stop a falling piece it needs something to hit: give the slabs' instanced mesh component collision, or add one box under the platform.

  9. Add steam. Add "Niagara" to the module's dependencies. Author NS_Steam with a user float parameter for the particle count, off-white, short-lived, rising. A puff spawns it with a small count, a burst with a large one.

  10. Tell the flow. Bind to the piece hands' two events. On OnActed, the first grab of a level calls Flow.BoardTouched() to start the clock. On OnBoardChanged, call Flow.SaveProgress(Board), and if the board's last win result says IsWin, call Flow.CompleteLevel(). Set bAcceptsGrabs each frame: true only in Play, with a board loaded, while the handle is not carrying the board.

Acceptance checks

  • All tests under Tsugi.Core.PieceDrop, BoardPick, TrayDock and ThrowGesture pass.
  • I can pinch each of the six tray pieces, with either hand, and with a controller's grip.
  • The ghost is green exactly where a release lands the piece, and red exactly where it comes back.
  • Raising the piece more than 10 cm above the board hides the ghost; letting go there drops it into the room.
  • Lifting a piece I laid empties the cell at once and the clues update.
  • A fixed piece wobbles and stays.
  • A dropped piece bounces on my real table and puffs after about three seconds; a thrown one bursts.
  • A piece that falls onto the board puffs at once and does not lie there.
  • Both hands can hold a piece at the same time.
  • A pinch on the handle's rail at the corner does not lift the corner cell's piece.
  • Walking round the table moves the tray to my side after about a second and a half, and never while I hold a piece.
  • I solved Ashgate by hand and the log shows Play → TrainRun.

Pitfalls

  • Six centimetres is small. The Unity project found hand precision at this size to be its main risk. If pinches miss, raise ReachCm a little before touching anything else, and check the tray's 1.3-cell pitch survived your port.
  • Hover changes the board. It must not. Clues update on release, never while a piece hovers.
  • A dangling board reference. FTsugiPieceDrop refers to the display's board. Reset it in End(), before the board is cleared.
  • A stale callback. A piece flying back to its cell takes a quarter of a second. If the level changes meanwhile, the landing callback must not touch the new board. Unity checks that the board is still the same object; capture a generation counter and compare it.
  • Physics scale. The held piece carries the board's 0.06 scale. A body that small and light behaves oddly with default settings. If it jitters, give the body a fixed mass override.

Knowledge check

You lift a piece from cell (2,3), carry it over cell (4,1) where its key is illegal, and let go. Meanwhile nothing else changed. What happens?

  • It puffs and is gone.
  • It flies back to (2,3) and is placed there again; the outcome is Returned with BoardChanged true.
  • It stays in the hand.

SendBack re-seats a lifted piece in its cell of origin when that is still legal. Only if the board changed around it, so it no longer fits, does it puff.

Why does the falling piece have a box as its root and the mesh as a child?

  • The baked track mesh has no collision shape, and a rigid body needs one; the box is also made thicker than the mesh so it cannot slip through a surface.
  • Static meshes cannot simulate physics.
  • So the mesh can be hidden.

A static mesh component can simulate physics if its mesh has simple collision. These do not, and the Unity code adds a box with a minimum thickness for the same reason.

The held piece is shown 1.2 cm below the pinch point. Which point decides the cell it hovers over?

  • The pinch point.
  • The piece's own centre, 1.2 cm below the pinch.
  • The fingertip.

Follow computes the piece's position first and picks the cell from that, so the ghost sits under what the player sees, not under their knuckles.
