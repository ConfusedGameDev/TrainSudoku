Wrist menu and pause

The player needs a way to pause, go back, and change settings without a ray and without a button floating in their view. The answer is a small roundel worn like a watch. You build it, the panel it opens, and the pause that must also happen when the headset comes off.

At the end
A wrist roundel that shows on a watch-check, a world-locked menu panel with a settings page, and a pause on every kind of focus loss.
On the headset
Turn your wrist, press the roundel, and the game pauses with a menu. Take the headset off mid-level; put it back on to a paused board.
Unity equivalent
Milestone XR8, "Wrist menu and pause".

Unreal concepts

Application lifecycle

Unity calls OnApplicationPause and OnApplicationFocus on your script. Unreal raises global delegates on FCoreDelegates that anything can bind to:

DelegateWhenUnity equivalent
ApplicationWillDeactivateDelegateFocus is lost: the system menu opens, the headset comes offOnApplicationFocus(false)
ApplicationHasReactivatedDelegateFocus returnsOnApplicationFocus(true)
ApplicationWillEnterBackgroundDelegateThe app is about to be suspendedOnApplicationPause(true)
ApplicationHasEnteredForegroundDelegateThe app is backOnApplicationPause(false)
ApplicationWillTerminateDelegateThe app is closingOnApplicationQuit

TrapOn mobile platforms these can be raised on a thread that is not the game thread. Game objects, the flow and delegates that update UI must only be touched on the game thread. Hop across with AsyncTask(ENamedThreads::GameThread, …) and capture a weak pointer, never this.

Hand joints as transforms

The wrist joint from lesson 9 is a full transform, with an orientation. Which of its axes points out of the back of the hand depends on how the runtime and the engine define the joint, and the Unity project itself records its own assumption about this as not yet verified. You will measure it.

Showing and hiding world widgets

The Unity code learned that switching a UI document off discards what was built into it, and hides panels by visibility instead. An Unreal widget component keeps its widget when hidden, so SetVisibility(false) is safe. The matching trap is ordering: set a panel's transform before making it visible, or it draws for one frame wherever it last was.

Steps

  1. Port the two rule files and their tests. WatchCheck is small enough to show whole.

    It takes directions, not transforms, so it is indifferent to axis conventions and its tests port unchanged.

  2. Find the wrist's axes. Add a debug draw of the wrist joint's coordinate frame with DrawDebugCoordinateSystem, put the headset on, and look at your wrist. Write down which axis, and which sign, points out of the back of the hand, and which points towards the fingers. Put the answer in one function, BackOfWrist(const FTransform&), with a comment saying you measured it. Check both hands: a mirrored hand may flip a sign.

  3. Build the roundel. A UWidgetComponent with a 160-pixel round widget, scaled to 3 cm, on an actor that follows the wearing hand's wrist each frame. Run the watch check with the direction from step 2 and the direction from the roundel to the camera. Give it a press component from lesson 12, with:

    • IgnoredHand set to the wearing hand;
    • bCanPress false for the first 0.3 seconds after it appears;
    • slop of 8 mm and an arm margin of 1.5 cm, because it rides a moving wrist;
    • Disarm() whenever it appears.

    The wearing hand is the non-dominant one: Preferences.GetWristHand(). With controllers and no tracked wrist, there is no roundel; the menu button is the way in.

  4. Build the panel. A second widget component: 640 pixels wide, 20 cm in the room, with a header and a column of large buttons. It has two pages, main and settings. On open, place it 6 cm above the roundel, turn it to face the eyes, and leave it there. Record which hand it opened over and set that as its press component's ignored hand until it closes.

    The panel raises events and decides nothing: OnToggled from the roundel or menu button, OnChosen(Item) for an entry, OnReplaceBoard, OnNudgeBoard(Centimetres). Hand, volumes and language are written straight to the preferences.

  5. Let the shell decide.

    Add one line to HandleStateChanged from lesson 13: every change of state closes the menu. And in the shell's Tick: the roundel is offered only when FTsugiWristMenuPlan::Offered(State).

  6. Pause on focus loss. Bind in the subsystem, since it outlives levels.

    SaveNow needs the board, which the shell owns. Give the subsystem a TFunction<const FTsugiBoard*()> the shell sets, the same kind of seam as the star times. Remove the three handles in Deinitialize.

    There is deliberately no handler for regaining focus. The game stays paused until the player presses RESUME, on the wrist menu or on the sign.

    Verify on the deviceWhich of these delegates fires for each event on Quest (headset removed, system menu opened, app switched away) was not confirmed for 5.8 with the Meta plugin. Log each one and try all three events. If taking the headset off raises none of them, look at FCoreDelegates::VRHeadsetRemovedFromHead and Meta's function library for input focus.

  7. Lock the board while paused. In Pause: the board is dimmed (lesson 7), bAcceptsGrabs is false (lesson 13), and the handle is unavailable. A paused board cannot be changed or moved.

Acceptance checks

  • Tsugi.Core.WristMenuPlan and WatchCheck tests pass.
  • The roundel appears when I look at the back of my wrist and is absent while I lay track.
  • Rolling my wrist slowly through the limit does not make the roundel flicker.
  • Turning my wrist into view while my other hand is near does not open the menu.
  • In play, pressing the roundel pauses the game and opens the menu; the clock stops and the board dims.
  • Pressing the roundel again closes the menu and the game stays paused.
  • RESUME, RETRY and BACK TO LINE MAP each do what they say, once.
  • The panel stays put when I move my wrist away, and closes when I walk a little over a metre from it.
  • Swapping the dominant hand in settings moves the tray and the roundel to the other side.
  • Raise and lower move the board by a centimetre a press, and never push it below the table.
  • Re-place board runs first placement again and returns to the same paused level.
  • Taking the headset off during play, waiting, and putting it back on shows a paused board with my pieces intact.
  • The left controller's menu button opens and closes the same menu.

Pitfalls

  • The store requires this. VRC.Quest.Functional.2: a single-player app must pause when the system asks. VRC.Quest.Input.4: an app must be focus-aware, hiding hands and controllers and ignoring input while unfocused. Lesson 18 checks both.
  • Saving on the way out. The board is already saved after every change. The only thing a hard kill can lose is a few seconds on the clock. Do not add blocking work to the background handler to chase that.
  • A press that opens and closes. The roundel's one-second cooldown is longer than the sign's 0.35 seconds on purpose: it rides a moving wrist and the finger lingers.
  • Menu state after a level change. Because every state change closes the menu, "open as the pause" must open it after calling PauseGame, not before.

Knowledge check

The menu is open over a paused game. The player presses the roundel. What state is the flow in afterwards?

  • Play
  • Pause
  • LevelSelect

Toggle with the menu open returns Close, and closing never resumes. A stray touch must not restart the clock.

Why does the lifecycle handler capture a TWeakObjectPtr and post to the game thread?

  • The callback may run on another thread, and by the time the posted task runs the subsystem may be gone.
  • Lambdas cannot capture this in Unreal.
  • It makes the pause happen sooner.

Touching game objects off the game thread is a data race. A raw this in a queued task is a use-after-free waiting for an app shutdown.

The watch check shows at 45° and hides at 65°. The wrist is held steady at 55°. Is the roundel visible?

  • Yes.
  • No.
  • It depends on whether it was already showing.

Between the two limits the check keeps its previous answer. That gap is what prevents flicker.
